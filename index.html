Tilt mode

The gesture you already make when someone leans in.

Tilt the phone past your threshold and a full-screen veil drops instantly. Straighten up and it clears just as fast. Nothing to tap.

Set the threshold where it suits you — 5 levels, from a firm 25° lean down to a 9° nudge, 17° by default. Pick which way counts: left, right, both sides, forward, back, or front-and-back. Then hold the phone the way you actually hold it and calibrate to your own grip in three seconds.

● Live preview shows the darkness before you switch it on
Pattern mode

Or lay a texture over the screen and flick it with one thumb.

Prefer a cover you control by hand? Pattern mode puts Bayer, Noise, Dither or Gradient over the screen, with darkness and opacity you tune to taste.

A small bubble floats on top of everything. Tap it to toggle the pattern, drag it to tuck it against whichever edge is out of your way.

● Independent of Tilt mode — run either, or both
Per-app filtering

Cover the banking app. Leave the map alone.

By default the veil covers everything. Open the picker, search your installed apps, and choose exactly which ones deserve it.

This needs Usage Access, and Tilt Veil uses it for one thing: reading which app is in front, on your device, and nothing else. Don't grant it and the app says so on screen — it falls back to covering every app instead of quietly pretending your selection worked.

● Never reads screen content, never keeps a history
Always in reach

It keeps working after you leave the app — and says so.

A foreground service keeps the veil alive once you switch away, which is the whole point: it has to cover whatever you're doing next.

So there's a persistent notification with Pause and Resume right on it. You always know when protection is on, and standing it down is one tap — never a guess.

How it works

Install. Turn on. Tilt.

Turn it on

One button on the main screen. Android asks for "Display over other apps" the first time — that's what lets the veil draw over whatever you're reading.

Set your threshold

Drag the sensitivity slider and pick a trigger direction. The preview tile shows how dark the screen will actually go, before you commit.

Forget about it

Go use your phone. When someone settles in beside you and you angle the screen away, the veil is already down.

How it's built

A privacy screen, not a security product.

Everything stays on the device

FAQ
Does this hide my screen from screenshots or screen recording?

No. Tilt Veil dims and covers the screen against people looking at it in person. It does not use FLAG_SECURE and makes no attempt to block capture — if you need that, this is the wrong tool.

Why does it need "Display over other apps"?

The veil is drawn as a system overlay on top of whatever app you're in. Without that permission there is nowhere to draw it, so protection cannot turn on at all.

Why does it ask for Usage Access?

Only if you choose to protect specific apps rather than all of them. It reads the package name of the app currently in front so it knows whether to show the veil, processes it on-device, and discards it. It never reads screen content and never keeps a history. Skip the permission and protection simply applies everywhere — the app tells you so instead of silently ignoring your choice.

Why is there a permanent notification?

Android requires a visible notification for any service that keeps running in the background. That's a feature here: it carries Pause and Resume, so the app can never be protecting — or not protecting — without you knowing.

Does it drain the battery?

It listens to the rotation sensor and draws a single overlay window. There is no network activity, no location, no camera, and no polling loop.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no in-app purchases, no subscription, no account. Every feature is available from the first launch.
